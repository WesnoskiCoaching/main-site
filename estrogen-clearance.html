The Estrogen Clearance Guide

Cycle symptoms deserve context, not a universal detox.

How we connect cycle timing, digestion, recovery and hormone results, and where medical assessment comes first.

Reviewed October 3, 2026 · Educational guide

01 of 08

Start with the cycle and the symptoms

Heavy bleeding, breast tenderness, irregular cycles and changes in mood or sleep are worth recording. They do not, by themselves, prove excess estrogen or poor clearance. At Wesnoski, we review the cycle, medication, food intake, digestion and recovery together.

Record when symptoms happen, the first day of bleeding, how heavy it is and whether contraception or hormone treatment changed. Persistent symptoms need clinical assessment for their possible causes.

Do not wait for a coaching review

Seek urgent care for very heavy bleeding with dizziness, faintness, chest pain or shortness of breath. New bleeding after menopause needs prompt assessment. Discuss unexplained absence of periods for more than three months with a clinician.

Evidence: ACOG: abnormal uterine bleeding · ACOG: absence of periods.

02 of 08

What “clearance” describes

Our framework separates hormone metabolism into modification, conjugation and excretion. The liver modifies estrogens and attaches groups that help their handling. Metabolites leave through urine and bile; some intestinal metabolites can be reabsorbed.

That physiology does not show which step explains a particular person’s symptoms. It also does not mean everyone needs a supplement for each step, or that a urine-metabolite ratio establishes cancer risk or the cause of PMS.

Where MARCH fits

We address nourishment and recovery, then relevant digestive concerns, while cycle-specific investigation proceeds when indicated. This is the Wesnoski coaching framework, not a validated test of estrogen clearance.

Evidence: Human estrogen-metabolism study (1976) · Estradiol recirculation study (1998).

03 of 08

Ask what the tests can answer

Questions before interpreting hormone or liver results
QuestionUseful distinction
Was ovulation assessed at the right time?If a progesterone test is indicated, roughly one week before the next expected period is more useful than automatically choosing day 21. Irregular cycles need an individual plan.
Can one progesterone level establish quality?A level above 3 ng/mL can provide evidence of recent ovulation in the right context. Progesterone fluctuates; one value cannot reliably grade luteal-phase quality.
Does a progesterone:estradiol ratio diagnose the problem?There is no validated universal ratio used here to diagnose “estrogen dominance.” Mixing ng/mL and pg/mL without conversion also changes the arithmetic by a factor of 1,000.
Do low liver-test values prove slow detox?ALT, AST, ALP and bilirubin need clinical interpretation. Low numerical cutoffs are not established tests of estrogen clearance or bile flow.

Evidence: ASRM: fertility evaluation (2021) · ASRM: luteal-phase assessment · ACG: evaluating liver chemistries.

For example, progesterone 10 ng/mL equals 10,000 pg/mL. Dividing by estradiol 100 pg/mL gives 100 using matching mass units, not 0.1. Correct arithmetic still does not turn the ratio into a validated diagnosis.

04 of 08

Carrots can be food without being a protocol

Raw or cooked carrots can be part of a varied diet if you enjoy and tolerate them. A human feeding study examined carrot fibre and bowel-related outcomes; it did not establish a raw-carrot treatment for high estrogen or cycle symptoms.

Constipation deserves attention, but requiring everyone to have two or three bowel movements every day is not a validated estrogen-clearance rule. Stool consistency, straining, discomfort and your usual pattern matter. The gut guide helps you record those details.

Evidence: Carrot-fibre feeding study (1994) · NIDDK: constipation symptoms and warning signs.

05 of 08

A mechanism is not a treatment result

DIM, calcium-D-glucarate, NAC and bile-support products appear in our educational material. A proposed pathway or a change in a laboratory biomarker does not establish that a combined stack treats your symptoms.

In a randomized trial involving people taking tamoxifen, DIM changed estrogen-metabolite measures but also reduced some tamoxifen metabolites. That is a reason for medication review, not a reason to add DIM independently. The study did not demonstrate that DIM cures PMS or reduces cancer events.

Evidence: DIM and tamoxifen randomized trial (2017).

A useful supplement question

What specific problem are we addressing, what human evidence supports this product for that problem, and how will we measure benefit or harm? Review existing products with your clinician or pharmacist before adding more.

06 of 08

Progesterone needs an individual prescription

Progesterone products differ by formulation, route and indication. Evidence and dosing for a prescribed oral capsule cannot be transferred to a drop product used under the tongue or on the skin.

We do not provide a universal Progest-E dose or day-14-to-28 schedule here. Discuss the symptom, cycle pattern, pregnancy possibility and any estrogen treatment with the prescriber. Do not start, stop or adjust hormone treatment from this guide.

Evidence: Progesterone capsule prescribing information · ASRM: luteal-phase assessment.

07 of 08

Keep nourishment and tolerance in view

In MARCH, enough food and a sustainable meal rhythm come before adding complexity. Review whether restriction has made meals smaller, less varied or harder to manage. Choose foods you tolerate and address constipation without an automatic list of banned foods.

We can help you record meals, bowel symptoms and cycle changes together. That record can guide a focused discussion, but a single reaction after a food does not prove an estrogen or liver problem. Avoid changing several foods and supplements at once if you want to understand what made a difference.

08 of 08

Your next-cycle record

  1. Record cycle dates, bleeding pattern and symptoms with their timing.
  2. Note contraception, prescribed hormones and supplements without changing them for the diary.
  3. Record sleep, energy, meals and bowel comfort so the context stays visible.
  4. Bring results with units and cycle timing. Ask what question each proposed test will answer.
  5. Agree on review and escalation with your clinician rather than treating a ratio or a temperature reading.
