Why You’re Still Bloated

What to look at before adding another supplement.

Recurring bloating, constipation or changing bowel habits deserve a clearer pattern. Start with what happens, when it happens and what has already been tried.

Reviewed October 3, 2026 · Educational guide

01 of 08

Start with what is actually happening

At Wesnoski, gut work begins with the wider picture: food intake, sleep, energy, medications, previous investigations and bowel function. We use that context to decide what needs attention first, rather than giving everyone the same gut stack.

Bloating is a feeling of pressure or fullness; visible swelling is distention. Either can occur for several reasons. The timing after a meal is worth recording, but it cannot diagnose low stomach acid, SIBO, Candida, “leaky gut” or impaired bile flow.

Evidence: AGA bloating guidance (2023).

When to seek medical help

Seek urgent care for severe or constant abdominal pain, vomiting with inability to pass gas or stool, fainting, or black, tar-like stool. Blood in stool, fever, unexplained weight loss or a persistent new change in bowel habits needs prompt medical assessment. Do not wait to complete a diary or a coaching phase.

Evidence: NIDDK: constipation symptoms and warning signs.

02 of 08

A simple seven-day record

Use this as a short observation window if symptoms are stable. Keep your usual pattern where practical; do not remove several foods just to make the diary look better. Record any changes that are medically needed.

Copyable symptom-and-bowel worksheet
What to recordYour daily notes
Meals and timingWhen you ate, the main foods and approximate portions; note unusually long gaps or rushed meals.
Bloating and painWhen it started, how long it lasted, severity from 0–10 and whether the abdomen visibly changed.
Bowel movementsNumber, hard/lumpy, formed or loose/watery stool; straining, pain, urgency or incomplete emptying.
ContextSleep, stress, activity, cycle timing if relevant, and new or changed medicines or supplements.
What you triedThe product or change, date started and what happened. Include changes that made no difference.

Copy these headings into a notebook or print this page. There is no health-data submission in this worksheet. Bring the record to your clinician or coaching review.

Look for repetition

One difficult meal is a clue to record. A repeated pattern is more useful, and still needs context before calling a food or a diagnosis the cause.

Download the seven-day worksheet to keep your notes privately on your own device or on paper.

03 of 08

Start with a few manageable actions

  1. Keep nourishment in view. In our M phase, we review whether you are eating enough and whether restriction has made meals difficult. Do not start with a fast or broad elimination list.
  2. Make room for meals. Sit down where possible, eat at a comfortable pace and chew food well. Record whether rushing seems to change symptoms rather than treating a chewing count as a cure.
  3. Make bowel habits practical. Allow unhurried toilet time, respond to the urge, and discuss persistent straining or incomplete emptying.
  4. Keep fluids and movement appropriate. Drink regularly within any medical fluid restrictions and include gentle activity you can manage. More is not always better.
  5. Change one optional thing at a time. That makes your response easier to interpret. Leave medication changes to the prescriber.

Evidence: NIDDK: constipation treatment.

Fibre, fermented foods and probiotics need a separate decision based on symptoms, tolerance and the purpose of the intervention. The next sections explain why.

04 of 08

What digestion involves

Breaking food down

Chewing, stomach processes, pancreatic enzymes and bile have different roles. The small intestine absorbs most nutrients. Gas or discomfort alone does not tell you which process is impaired.

Moving contents through

Gut movement, stool consistency and the mechanics of emptying all matter. Constipation can include difficult or incomplete passage, even when someone goes regularly.

Microbes and gut–brain signalling

Microbes help process some food components. Sensitivity and gut–brain communication can also influence discomfort. Symptoms are real even when a routine scan does not explain them.

Evidence: NIDDK: how digestion works · AGA bloating guidance (2023).

Reflux involves stomach contents moving into the oesophagus. It is not proof of too little stomach acid. An acid-supplement challenge is not a reliable home diagnosis, and prescribed reflux treatment should be reviewed with the clinician.

Evidence: ACG reflux guideline (2022).

The Wesnoski sequence

Our MARCH framework asks what the person can sustain before adding more complex work. It is a coaching sequence, not a scientifically validated diagnostic pathway or a requirement that everyone receive every treatment.

MARCH as a coaching framework
PhaseWhat we review
M · MitochondriaNourishment, energy, sleep, recovery and relevant nutrient findings. We establish a manageable foundation.
A · Absorption + DetoxDigestion, bowel pattern, food tolerance and relevant clinical findings. The name does not imply a universal cleanse or antimicrobial course.
R · ResilienceStress response, sleep and recovery; whether the plan remains tolerable as daily demands change.

H · Healthspan / HypertrophySustainable activity, strength and longer-term habits while monitoring the earlier concerns.

We review readiness using energy and sleep stability, bowel comfort, tolerance of food and the direction of relevant results. A calendar date alone does not decide a phase change. If the person is deteriorating, we reassess rather than intensifying the plan.

Medical care does not wait for a gate

We use practice-specific laboratory and symptom targets to organise coaching. These are not validated medical clearance tests. Suspected infection, bleeding, significant deficiency or other concerning findings need timely clinical care regardless of phase.

06 of 08

Why previous attempts may not have helped

More fibre is not one intervention

Type, amount and tolerance matter. A randomized IBS trial found benefit from psyllium, while bran was less helpful. A sudden increase can be difficult to tolerate; ongoing constipation still needs assessment.

A probiotic is not a general bloating treatment

Products and indications differ. The AGA does not recommend probiotics specifically to treat abdominal bloating and distention. A worsening response is not proof of “die-off.”

Restriction needs a purpose and an endpoint

A controlled low-FODMAP trial reduced symptoms in people with IBS. It does not justify permanent broad elimination for everyone. A supervised trial should include reintroduction and nutritional review.

Fermented foods are optional

A small trial in healthy adults found microbiome and immune-marker changes with a fermented-food diet. It did not establish treatment for SIBO or recurrent bloating. Tolerance and individual context still matter.

Evidence: Psyllium versus bran randomized trial (2009) · AGA bloating guidance (2023) · Halmos et al., low-FODMAP crossover trial (2014) · Wastyk et al., fermented-food trial (2021).

We select gut support for a defined reason and review the response. That does not mean every reader should use the same combination. Bring the full list of what you have tried, including doses and dates.

07 of 08

Choose a test for a question

Questions to take to your clinician
Possible clinical questionWhat to know
Could coeliac disease contribute?A clinician may use blood tests and, when indicated, further investigation. Discuss testing before starting a gluten-free diet.
Is a breath test useful?Hydrogen or methane testing may help selected patients. It has limitations and should be interpreted with symptoms and risk factors, rather than ordered for every bloated person.
Is emptying the bowel the issue?Persistent difficulty evacuating may warrant pelvic-floor assessment. A microbiome report does not answer that question.
Would stool testing change care?Targeted tests may be useful for a specific clinical concern. Broad commercial microbiome profiles have limited established clinical utility and do not provide a universal personalised treatment prescription.

Evidence: AGA bloating guidance (2023) · International microbiome-testing consensus (2025).

Which result would change the plan? What would a normal result mean? Those questions help keep investigation useful. We do not diagnose an infection, low acid or a permeability disorder from symptom timing or a commercial “dysbiosis” label alone.

08 of 08

What an individual plan adds

We bring the diary together with the wider history: nourishment, medication, previous diagnoses and tests, sleep, energy, menstrual changes where relevant and the response to earlier attempts. That helps us choose a manageable next step and identify questions for your clinician.

The plan should explain why each action is being used, what response we are looking for, how long to assess it and when to reconsider. Progress is more comfortable and sustainable daily function, not simply reaching a product count or a calendar week.

If oral iron changed your bowel habits, read the iron guide. If symptoms began around a medicine change, Depleted can help organise the review. Neither is a reason to alter a prescription yourself.
