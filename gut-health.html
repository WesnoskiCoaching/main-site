Why You’re Still Bloated

Stomach acid, enzymes, bile, motility and the sequence behind our gut work.

Gut support is more than adding a probiotic. Food has to be broken down, mixed with digestive secretions, moved through the intestine and absorbed. The lining and microbial environment matter too. Our framework connects those functions with energy, nourishment and recovery before deciding what to add next.

01 of 08

Start upstream: the meal and the foundation

Digestion begins before food reaches the stomach. Smell, taste, chewing and nervous-system activity all participate in preparing for a meal. We begin with how you eat, as well as what you eat.

If every meal happens standing up, rushing between tasks or while distracted, begin by making one meal easier to sit down for. Chew comfortably and give yourself enough time to eat. These are practical foundations, not a claim that chewing will resolve every gut condition.

MARCH also asks whether the person has the energy and nutritional foundation for more demanding work. Food restriction, poor sleep, low iron or other relevant deficiencies can change what is tolerable. We address that foundation in the M phase before moving into more intensive gut work.

This does not mean ignoring gut symptoms until a calendar date. It means choosing the order with the whole person in view. Severe pain, bleeding, persistent vomiting or a significant deterioration needs timely assessment rather than waiting through a coaching phase.

02 of 08

Stomach acid and protein digestion

Stomach acid helps create the environment in which pepsin works on protein. The stomach also mixes the meal before moving contents onward. We start here because digestion begins well before food reaches the lower gut.

We review the meal pattern, early fullness, upper-abdominal discomfort, reflux history, medication and previous diagnoses. Those observations help form questions; they cannot establish low stomach acid by themselves.

We consider betaine HCl with pepsin for selected acid and protein-digestion support, and apple cider vinegar in appropriate circumstances. Neither establishes a diagnosis, and neither is something everyone with bloating needs.

Active gastritis, ulcers and acid-suppressing medication can rule out acid support. We do not instruct readers to keep increasing acid until it burns or to counteract a prescription without coordination.

It helps to distinguish the stomach stage from the later stages of digestion. If a plan keeps adding lower-gut products while overlooking the upper-GI history, it may be asking the wrong question.

03 of 08

Enzymes and bile have different jobs

Digestive enzymes act on different components of food. Proteases act on proteins, lipases on fats and amylases on starches. The pancreas contributes enzymes and bicarbonate to the small intestine. Bicarbonate helps neutralize the acidic contents leaving the stomach.

Bile is made by the liver and stored and released by the gallbladder. It helps disperse dietary fats so they can be processed and absorbed. Our framework therefore considers the fat-digestion picture separately from the protein-digestion picture.

We consider digestive enzymes, selected bile support and the meal itself. Ox bile and TUDCA have different uses; floating stools or discomfort alone do not establish that you need either.

Previous gallbladder surgery, pale or oily stools, meal-related pain, medication and existing diagnoses change the assessment. Your plan explains what each digestive support is intended to address and how we will monitor tolerance. You may not need acid, enzymes and bile support together.

04 of 08

Motility, the MMC and prokinetics

Breaking food down and moving it through are separate functions. Peristalsis moves contents along the digestive tract. The migrating motor complex, or MMC, is a pattern of movement that occurs between meals in the upper digestive tract. We pay close attention to this movement between meals.

Our review includes stool consistency, straining, incomplete emptying, frequency and the person’s usual meal rhythm. Someone can have a bowel movement every day and still report difficult or incomplete evacuation. More fibre does not answer every motility question.

Prokinetics are agents used to support gastrointestinal movement. Supporting motility is different from softening stool or repeatedly using a laxative. Options include ginger-based support and, through the appropriate clinician, prescription prokinetics. The choice depends on the problem being addressed and the person’s circumstances.

Meal structure, adequate food, appropriate fluids, daily movement and recovery are part of this layer. Meal spacing is considered in relation to the whole case; it should not become a reason to under-eat or ignore medication-related food needs.

We continue to support motility after targeted gut treatment. That is why our gut work does not end the moment an antimicrobial course ends. The long-term routine and the original contributors still need attention.

05 of 08

The lining and the microbial picture

The intestinal lining is an active barrier involved in absorption and interaction with the contents of the gut. We consider mucosal support alongside digestive function and the microbial picture.

Zinc carnosine, glutamine, DGL and slippery elm serve different purposes at different stages. We consider zinc carnosine particularly for the gastric lining, and glutamine during repair and transition work. These products do not all do the same job and should not be combined because their labels say “gut health.”

Targeted antimicrobial work is another category. It belongs to a selected case and a defined question, with a plan for tolerance and follow-up. We support the terrain first, then use targeted antimicrobial treatment if needed. Bloating alone is not a reason to assume an infection or start a kill protocol.

The rebuilding discussion includes food tolerance, microbial diversity and the ability to maintain regular meals. A worsening response needs reassessment; calling every flare “die-off” can conceal that the intervention is unsuitable.

06 of 08

How this fits into MARCH

M, mitochondria: establish the energy and nourishment foundation. We review relevant iron and nutrient findings, thyroid context, sleep and the ability to sustain the routine.

A, absorption and detox: examine digestion, acid and enzyme support where appropriate, bile, bowel movement, relevant gut findings and any selected targeted treatment. The content of this phase depends on the case.

R, resilience: strengthen recovery, nervous-system tolerance and the longer-term routine. Rebuilding tools such as glutamine and probiotics may fit here or during the transition from A to R, depending on the individual plan.

C, cycle and signal: bring relevant thyroid and sex-hormone context into the plan once the preceding foundation is workable. Cycle timing is part of interpreting the information.

H, healthspan and hypertrophy: build strength, capacity and sustainable habits while continuing to monitor the earlier concerns.

We use relevant results, trajectory and tolerance to decide when to move on, rather than elapsed time alone. A concerning medical finding needs investigation or treatment without waiting for a coaching phase.

07 of 08

Why “more gut supplements” can miss the problem

A probiotic does not replace stomach acid, digestive enzymes, bile or motility. That is the distinction behind our sequence. The question is what the person needs the next intervention to do.

Probiotic selection includes the strain, indication, phase and tolerance. S. boulardii and bacterial probiotics are different tools, with different timing around targeted treatment. The timing should be explained in the individual plan rather than assumed from the product category alone.

Prebiotics also need context. We may introduce PHGG gradually, then consider other fibres according to tolerance. Fermented foods are similarly selected rather than compulsory.

Restriction needs a next step. If a food is removed, record the reason and the plan for reassessment or reintroduction. Repeatedly removing more foods without a clear response can make nourishment harder and obscure the original problem.

08 of 08

What to do with the framework

Start by listing what you are currently taking and why: acid support, enzymes, bile support, motility support, probiotics, antimicrobials or lining support. If you cannot explain the purpose of an item, that is a useful question for the plan review. Do not change a prescribed medicine from this exercise.

Keep a short record of meals, symptom timing, bowel function and what changes when you add or remove an optional intervention. Use the record to connect the response with the systems and sequence we have covered.

Bring the response to previous attempts into the next decision. Did a product change bowel frequency but leave the meal discomfort unchanged? Did increased fibre make eating harder? Did symptoms return after a treatment ended? These distinctions help define what needs review instead of repeating the same stack.

A personalized Wesnoski plan explains the starting point, the role of each selected tool, how it fits with the phase, what progress would look like and when we would change direction. That is the difference between receiving a product list and understanding your plan.

If you have tried changing foods or adding supplements and still do not have a clear plan, we can review your digestion, bowel pattern, energy and previous attempts together.
