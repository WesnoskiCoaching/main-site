What the GI-MAP is

The GI-MAP is an at-home stool test that shows what's living in your gut and how well your gut is working. It's run by Diagnostic Solutions Laboratory and is one of the most widely used stool tests in functional health.

It reads DNA. The test uses qPCR to find the specific DNA of bacteria, yeast, parasites and viruses. Standard stool tests often rely on growing a culture or looking under a microscope, which can miss organisms that are hard to grow or present in small amounts.

It measures amounts, not just yes or no. You see how much of each organism is there, so your coach or provider can spot overgrowths and imbalances, not only infections.

It's one sample, collected at home. The kit includes everything you need, and the sample ships back at room temperature.

Signs your gut might be the missing piece

Gut problems don't always show up as stomach problems. If several of these sound like you, a GI-MAP may be worth talking about with your provider or coach.

Bloating, gas or discomfort after meals, even with "clean" food

Constipation, loose stools, or swinging between the two

Food sensitivities that keep growing

Low energy or brain fog that sleep doesn't fix

Skin issues like acne, eczema or rashes

Stubborn fat loss despite a solid plan

Bloodwork showing low iron, low B12 or inflammation with no clear cause

Autoimmune or thyroid issues

Symptoms that started after antibiotics, food poisoning or travel

Any of these can have other causes too. That's why testing beats guessing.

How to prep for the test

What you take before the test can hide what's really going on. Never stop a prescription without talking to your doctor first.

WhatWhen to stopWhy
AntibioticsWait 2 weeks after finishing, with your prescriber’s guidanceThey wipe out bacteria and skew the results
Oral steroids or immune suppressantsFor prednisone, wait 2 weeks after finishing. Other prescriptions continue unless your prescriber advises otherwiseThey can mask immune and inflammation markers
Antimicrobial herbs and supplementsInitial test: 2 weeks before. Retest: 4 weeks beforeThey can knock down organisms you're trying to find
ProbioticsSpore- or soil-based: 2 weeks before. Regular probiotics: 3 days beforeGives a true baseline instead of a reading of the supplement
Ox bile or lipase enzymes3 days beforeThey change the fat digestion readings
Biofilm disruptorsFor retesting: 4 weeks beforeAsk your coach
AspirinAnti-inflammatory medicines: 2 weeks before, only with your prescriber’s approvalCan affect the hidden blood marker
Colonoscopy or barium enemaWait at least 4 weeks afterThe prep clears out your gut

Keep taking your prescription medications unless your doctor tells you otherwise.

Before you collect

For initial testing, also discuss stopping bismuth 2 weeks before collection and other gut-specific supplements, including digestive supports and gut-healing formulas, 3 days before collection.

For the 3 days before collection, follow your coach’s instructions to eat 70–100 g of fat per day. If assessing your response to gluten, discuss the preparation with your provider before changing your intake.

Do not collect while menstruating. Write your name and date of birth on the vial and ship the sample as soon as possible using your kit’s instructions.

These timings distinguish an initial test from a retest. Confirm your preparation with your coach and prescribing clinician. Do not stop or change prescription medication on your own.

05 / 08

Why the order matters

Finding something on a GI-MAP is step one. Jumping straight into a harsh cleanse or "kill" protocol on an inflamed gut is how people flare up and quit. The R3 System works in order:

1. Regulate. Calm inflammation and stress first, so your body is ready to handle gut work.

2. Repair. Support digestion and the gut lining, then go after overgrowths and pathogens when your markers say you're ready.

3. Rebuild. Bring back healthy bacteria and lock in the habits that keep your gut stable.

Your results decide where you start. Not everyone needs the same protocol, and retesting shows whether it's working.
