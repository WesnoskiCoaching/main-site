The Complete Iron Protocol

Understand the pattern before changing the dose.

How we read ferritin, investigate a stalled response and fit iron treatment around food, symptoms and medication.

Reviewed October 3, 2026 · Educational guide

01 of 06

Read ferritin in context

At Wesnoski, we look at the person and the panel together: energy, food intake, menstrual losses, digestion, training and the trend over time. A ferritin result is useful, but it cannot answer every one of those questions.

What to discuss with your clinician
MarkerWhat it adds
FerritinA measure of iron stores. Inflammation can raise it, so a normal or high result does not always exclude iron deficiency.
CBC / haemoglobinWhether anaemia is present, plus red-cell size and other clues. Iron depletion can exist before anaemia.
Transferrin saturation / TIBCContext about circulating iron and its transport. These help when ferritin and symptoms do not agree.
CRP and clinical historyContext for inflammation; not a test that identifies its cause.

Evidence: BSG iron-deficiency guideline (2021).

A Wesnoski practice target

Our current coaching reference uses ferritin 70–100 ng/mL for women and 100–150 ng/mL for men. These are house targets, not universal diagnostic thresholds or instructions to take iron. Pregnancy, inflammation, ongoing losses and medical conditions change interpretation.

Iron-dependent thyroid peroxidase helps make thyroid hormone. The deiodinase enzymes involved in thyroid-hormone metabolism are selenium-containing enzymes. This distinction does not establish a ferritin level below which thyroid medication stops working. Animal mechanism studies cannot set a human medication threshold.

Evidence: Hess et al., animal mechanism study (2002) · Berry et al., enzyme study (1991).

02 of 06

Five questions when the result stalls

  1. Are stores depleted, or is iron less available during inflammation? These can coexist. The distinction affects the treatment plan.
  2. Is iron still being lost? Heavy menstrual bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding and blood donation belong in the history.
  3. Is there enough intake, and is absorption impaired? A clinician may investigate conditions such as coeliac disease when indicated. Bloating alone does not diagnose low stomach acid.
  4. What is actually being taken? Bring the product, elemental-iron amount, frequency, missed doses and side effects. A label’s total compound weight is not the elemental-iron dose.
  5. Is there a response in the right marker? Haemoglobin, ferritin and symptoms do not necessarily change at the same speed. Agree on follow-up rather than repeatedly adding products.
Get medical help promptly

Chest pain, fainting, breathlessness at rest, vomiting blood or black, tar-like stool need urgent assessment. New or unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia needs a cause investigated, even when a supplement seems to help.

Evidence: BSG iron-deficiency guideline (2021).

03 of 06

What oral-iron evidence can tell us

Our current approach treats tolerance and consistency as part of the plan. A clinician chooses the formulation, elemental dose and schedule using the severity and cause of deficiency, other conditions and your response.

Daily or alternate-day?

A 2017 trial in iron-depleted women found higher fractional absorption with alternate-day dosing under its study conditions. This means a larger proportion of the tested dose was absorbed; it does not mean daily iron is unabsorbed. A later trial in people with iron-deficiency anaemia found no significant difference in haemoglobin improvement between its daily and alternate-day groups.

Vitamin C is optional

Vitamin C can improve absorption of non-haem iron in food. In the 2020 treatment trial, oral iron alone was equivalent to iron plus vitamin C for the studied outcomes. A vitamin C tablet is not a universal requirement with every iron dose.

Lactoferrin is a separate question

Early studies, including pregnancy research, suggest possible benefits, but the evidence does not establish lactoferrin as an equivalent replacement for iron repletion across patients. Depleted stores and inflammation-related iron restriction need to be distinguished before considering it.

Evidence: Stoffel et al., absorption trial (2017) · Daily versus alternate-day clinical trial (2023) · Li et al., randomized trial (2020) · Paesano et al., pregnancy trial (2006).

Guidance is not unanimous: the AGA’s 2024 expert advice still recommends adding vitamin C. Our optional wording reflects the randomized trial and the distinction between dietary absorption and treatment outcomes. Discuss the choice with the treating clinician.

Evidence: AGA iron-deficiency guidance (2024).

Keep medication timing separate

Timing questions for your pharmacist
ItemWhat to check
Levothyroxine tabletsThe cited label specifies an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast. Keep iron and calcium supplements at least four hours apart from the tablet.
Coffee and breakfastFollow the instructions for your particular thyroid product and ask your pharmacist about coffee. The breakfast interval does not replace the four-hour supplement separation.
Other thyroid formulationsLiquid, soft-gel, liothyronine and combination products need their own instructions. Do not apply one tablet schedule to every thyroid medicine.
Your iron productAsk about food, calcium, tea, coffee and other medicines when choosing a workable schedule. Tolerance matters; do not repeatedly skip doses because an idealised schedule is impossible.

Evidence: Levothyroxine tablet prescribing information · BSG iron-deficiency guideline (2021).

05 of 06

When iron upsets your gut

Write down what happens: nausea, pain, constipation or diarrhoea, when it starts, and which product you took. Your prescriber can consider a different preparation or schedule, and intravenous iron when appropriate. Persistent symptoms or poor response deserve reassessment, not an automatic switch to lactoferrin.

Acid-blocking medicines may affect iron absorption in some circumstances. They also have valid treatment roles. Ask for a medication review rather than stopping one or trying an acid supplement to “test” your stomach.

Evidence: BSG iron-deficiency guideline (2021) · ACG reflux guideline (2022).

The gut guide includes a symptom worksheet. Depleted explains the medication interaction in more detail.

Evidence: AGA iron-deficiency guidance (2024).

06 of 06

Agree on the next check

Follow-up depends on the severity and cause. In iron-deficiency anaemia, the BSG guideline recommends checking the haemoglobin response within the first four weeks. A later iron-panel review can assess replenishment. Do not wait twelve weeks to mention worsening symptoms or a treatment you cannot tolerate.

Our coaching review follows both the laboratory trend and daily function. Bring the original results, dates, dose, timing and any changes in bleeding or digestion. The clinician decides when treatment is complete or maintenance is needed. Menstruation does not automatically mean lifelong supplementation.

Evidence: BSG iron-deficiency guideline (2021).

Bring these questions

What explains the deficiency? Which result will show response? When should we recheck? What should I do if the treatment causes symptoms?
