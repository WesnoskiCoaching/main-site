Accutane (Roaccutane): what it is & why it’s prescribed

Accutane (isotretinoin) is a powerful oral retinoid derived from vitamin A. It is prescribed as a last resort for moderate-to-severe acne that has not responded to antibiotics or topical treatments. It works by dramatically shrinking the sebaceous (oil) glands, reducing sebum production, limiting bacterial growth and decreasing inflammation.

Conventional dosing protocol: A typical course runs 16–24 weeks at 0.5–1mg per kg of body weight per day, titrated based on response and tolerability. The goal is a cumulative dose of around 120–150mg/kg, after which many dermatologists consider the course "complete." Most patients see significant clearing during treatment.

The question is: what happens after?

Why a functional approach doesn’t encourage this route

Conventional medicine measures success as clearance of acne ... here's where functional medicine asks a deeper question: why is the body producing this acne in the first place? Accutane does not address root cause - it suppresses the symptom. And it does so at a significant cost to the body.

  • Extreme dryness of skin, lips & eyes
  • Severe joint & muscle pain
  • Chronic fatigue & brain fog
  • Disrupted sleep quality
  • Mood changes & depression risk
  • Gut microbiome disruption
  • Hair thinning & loss
  • Hormonal disruption

The rebound reality and what to do now

Coming off Accutane doesn't mean your acne journey is over. For many, it's just the beginning of the next chapter. Here's what the research tells us ... and more importantly, what you can do about it.

Rebound happens because Accutane never addressed the reason your skin was producing excess sebum and inflammation in the first place. Once the drug clears your system, the underlying drivers - hormonal imbalance, gut dysfunction, nutrient deficiencies - just re-introduce themselves back into the body. The oil glands regrow. The cycle begins again.

Hormonal disruption is a central driver of rebound!! Isotretinoin alters androgen signalling during treatment. When it's withdrawn, hormone levels can surge erratically - spiking sebum production, triggering inflammation, and restarting the acne cascade. The chance of being represcribed a second course sits at around 20–30%, but outcomes are often less predictable, and the cumulative systemic burden on the body increases significantly.
