The Iron Reset

Food, daily habits and the wider picture of your iron.

A guide to prepare better questions and build a more informed daily plan.

Reviewed October 3, 2026 · Educational guide

01 of 06

Start with the wider picture

Chan’s Iron Reset starts with nourishment and understanding. Before adding another supplement, bring together your symptoms, food intake, existing results and anything you have already tried.

Tiredness alone does not tell you that iron is low. A useful review asks what else is happening: menstrual bleeding, food restriction, digestive symptoms, medication changes and recovery demands.

A practical starting point

Write down your questions and bring the actual lab report, including units and reference ranges. Avoid changing a prescribed treatment from a single number.

02 of 06

What iron does

Iron is part of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells. Ferritin reflects iron stores but can rise with inflammation, so results need context from other measurements and your history.

Food contains heme and nonheme iron. Meat and seafood provide heme iron; beans, tofu and fortified foods offer nonheme sources. Both can have a place in a varied diet.

Read more: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: iron.

03 of 06

Build meals you can repeat

Choose an iron-containing food that fits your preferences and tolerance. For example, combine lentils with peppers, tofu with vegetables, or meat or seafood with a balanced meal.

Vitamin C-rich foods can improve nonheme iron absorption. That is a food-pairing option, not a requirement to take vitamin C with every iron tablet.

You do not need to organise every meal around avoiding other nutrients. Keep variety and adequate overall food intake in view.

Read more: NIH: vitamin C and nonheme iron.

Read more: Randomized trial of iron with or without vitamin C.

04 of 06

Keep the routine manageable

Chan’s daily rhythm is a way to make nutrition easier to repeat. Start with a breakfast you can manage, make time for lunch and choose an evening routine that supports rest.

Keep a brief note of energy, bowel comfort and anything that makes meals difficult. Review patterns over time with your coach instead of judging the plan by a single day.

Food first does not mean food only

An established deficiency may need treatment. Food habits support the plan; they do not replace investigation of its cause or a prescribed iron treatment.

05 of 06

Make supplements an individual decision

If iron is recommended, ask about the elemental dose, formulation, timing and follow-up. Bring up constipation, nausea or other difficulties early so your clinician can review the plan.

Organ supplements are not a required part of the Iron Reset. Product content varies, and a “wholefood” label does not establish the dose you need or make a supplement suitable for everyone.

Iron can interact with medicines. Levothyroxine tablets should be kept at least four hours apart from iron and calcium supplements; ask your pharmacist about your specific formulation and other medicines.

Read more: Levothyroxine tablet prescribing information.

For a fuller explanation of bloodwork, dosing questions and follow-up, read the Complete Iron Protocol.

06 of 06

Bring your questions to Chan

Bring your lab results, current food pattern, supplements and the changes you have noticed. Together, you can identify practical habits to work on and questions that need your clinician’s assessment.

A useful plan has a reason for each step and a review point. The aim is a routine that fits your life alongside appropriate medical care, without promising a fixed recovery timeline.

Do not wait on a coaching review

Severe breathlessness, chest pain or fainting needs urgent medical attention.
