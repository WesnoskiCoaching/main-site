The Iron Reset

Iron doesn’t work alone. Nourish the whole picture.

I’m so glad you’re here. Iron is one of the most talked-about nutrients and one of the most misunderstood.

Over the years, I’ve worked with many people who felt tired, flat, or just not themselves, even after being told their iron levels were “fine” or after trying supplements that didn’t seem to help. I’ve put together a simple-to-understand Iron Reset guide to offer a clearer, more holistic view of how iron really works in the body, and why it doesn’t act alone.

This guide brings together practical food tips, smart daily habits, and a wholefood-based approach to support your nutritional foundation. My hope is that you don’t just jump to trying yet another supplement but you understand how connected a food-first journey with iron really is.

Why you’re here

You might be here because you’re feeling a little off. Maybe your energy dips more than usual, your workouts feel harder than they should, or you’re just not feeling like yourself. Even if your iron levels appear “normal” on paper, you may still sense that something’s out of balance and you’re not alone in that.

Iron is a vital nutrient, but it’s not the only player in the system. For your body to use and manage iron effectively, it also needs a team of supporting nutrients, like vitamin A, B12, folate, and vitamin C. These cofactors help iron move through your body, assist in the production of red blood cells, and support the systems involved in oxygen transport and energy metabolism.

This isn’t a quick fix. It’s a food-first framework that brings iron and its nutrient partners together in the form your body recognizes best: whole foods, like grass-fed beef spleen.

Paired with education and ongoing support, this approach is designed to help you feel more in tune with your nutritional needs. This educational guide offers a practical daily protocol. It’s your guide to building better iron habits through real food, lifestyle awareness, and shared wisdom.

You’re here because you want to feel supported and informed. The Iron Reset is here to help you take that next step naturally.

Why iron alone isn’t enough

When tiredness sets in, or your bloodwork suggests your iron stores are on the lower end, the usual advice is simple: “take more iron.” But if you’ve tried that and haven’t noticed much change, you’re not alone. Many people supplement with iron and still don’t feel quite right.

That’s because iron doesn’t function in isolation. For your body to absorb, transport, and utilise iron effectively, it relies on a network of other nutrients working in harmony. Let’s take a closer look at a few of them:

Rather than isolating iron, The Iron Reset focuses on a wholefood-first approach that includes the natural cofactors your body uses to support nutrient synergy. By choosing a food-based source of iron, like freeze-dried beef spleen, you’re giving your body a complex of nutrients it recognises and can use, all in a balanced, gentle format that fits into your daily routine.

This approach isn’t about treating or fixing. It’s about supporting your nutrition through informed choices, consistency, and education so you can feel more confident about what you’re giving your body.

The nutrients that matter

01

Iron: the essential mineral

Iron plays a key role in your body’s oxygen delivery system. It forms the core of haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that helps carry oxygen throughout the body.

  • Supports oxygen transport via red blood cells
  • Contributes to normal energy production
  • Supports healthy cognitive and immune function

Best sources: Heme iron (from animal foods like beef spleen and liver) is more readily absorbed than non-heme iron from plant foods.

02

Vitamin A: iron’s transport partner

Vitamin A helps move stored iron into circulation and supports red blood cell development. It also contributes to the normal function of the immune system and plays a role in maintaining healthy tissues.

  • Assists iron mobilisation
  • Supports red blood cell maturation
  • Contributes to immune function

Best sources: Liver, egg yolks, cod liver oil.

03

Vitamin B12: the blood & nerve supporter

Vitamin B12 is necessary for the formation of red blood cells and the synthesis of DNA. It also supports normal neurological function and energy metabolism.

  • Contributes to red blood cell production
  • Supports brain and nerve function
  • Aids energy metabolism

Best sources: Liver, kidney, eggs, seafood.

04

Folate · vitamin B9: the cell growth supporter

Folate is important for normal cell division, especially in rapidly renewing tissues like those in the bone marrow. It also contributes to the formation of red blood cells.

  • Supports DNA synthesis and repair
  • Assists healthy tissue growth
  • Supports red blood cell development

Best sources: Leafy greens, liver, legumes.

Tips to improve iron day-to-day

Your journey starts with a single day, a chance to reset your approach and reconnect with the foundations of good nutrition, gentle movement, and consistent habits.

This isn’t about perfection or fixing everything at once. It’s about taking small, intentional steps that help your body get the most from the nutrients you’re giving it, especially iron.

Below is a simple day-long framework to support your routine and iron absorption.

01

Morning · wake

  • Start your day with a glass of warm lemon water or filtered water with a pinch of sea salt
  • Choose a protein-rich, whole food meal like eggs, avocado, and leafy greens
  • Spend 10–15 minutes in morning sunlight to support your natural body rhythms

Tip: Wait at least 1 hour between caffeine and your supplements. Caffeine is a diuretic and may affect nutrient absorption, particularly iron.

02

Midday · nourish

  • Choose a nutrient-dense lunch: think slow-cooked meat, root veggies, and fermented foods
  • Add a source of vitamin C (e.g., citrus, sauerkraut, capsicum) to help support iron uptake
  • Move your body gently: go for a walk, stretch, or do light activity

Gentle movement after a meal can support healthy blood sugar levels by helping direct energy to the muscles and liver, where it’s needed most.

03

Night · sleep

  • Have a light, blood sugar-friendly dinner (e.g., protein + slow carbs)
  • Wind down with calming practices like herbal tea, magnesium, or screen-free time
  • Aim for 8+ hours of restorative sleep to support your recovery and nutrient utilisation
05 of 06

Eat with purpose

When it comes to supporting iron through diet, what you eat, and when, can make a meaningful difference. It’s not just about taking a supplement. It’s about creating the right conditions for your body to absorb and use the nutrients you’re providing.

This section offers practical food guidance to help you get the most from your iron source and your wholefood-based approach.

Pair these with your iron

These foods may help support iron uptake when consumed as part of a balanced diet:

  • Vitamin C-rich foods: citrus, capsicum, strawberries, sauerkraut
  • Animal protein: beef, lamb, chicken, eggs
  • Healthy fats: avocado, ghee, olive oil (may support vitamin A absorption)
  • Liver or organ meats naturally rich in iron and its nutrient cofactors

Space these apart

Some foods and drinks may reduce iron absorption when taken at the same time. Try to space them at least 1–2 hours away from your main iron source.

  • Calcium-rich foods/supplements: milk, cheese, yoghurt, calcium tablets
  • Caffeine: coffee, black tea, energy drinks
  • Phytates and oxalates: found in grains, legumes, spinach, some nuts

You don’t have to give up these foods; just be mindful of timing for best results.

Best practices

  • Take your main iron source with a meal, following the directions on the label
  • Avoid coffee, tea, or dairy within 1–2 hours of your dose
  • Space your iron away from calcium supplements and high-fibre meals

Eating with purpose isn’t about restriction. It’s about timing and awareness, so your body can make the most of the nutrients you give it.
